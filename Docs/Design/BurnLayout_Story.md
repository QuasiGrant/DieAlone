# Old burn and forage: what they must hold (story)
Quill, 2026-10-02. **Draft** for 8.28; Sable places, Grant decides. L = layout, needs a place now. D = dressing, Milestone 11. Positions: Main3.md 2 (item 10) and 4 (route table); Valley.md 1.2, 1.3, 6 (E3, E4, E13, 6.1), 7.6, 8, 10; ForestPlan 8.2 and 8.4; DailyLoop 3. Deck lines are unverified until Rook's frames.

The burn. What it says: the fire already came this way once, from the road side, and was stopped at the camp. Everything grew back thick and short. It is the scar you walk through every time you go out for supplies, under open sky, with the tower watching over it. On day 1 it is the most ordinary thing on the map: an old burn under a lookout.

What burned, when (draft, Grant)
1. L. What burned: a strip of mixed forest from the road verge west to the knoll foot. The shape already tells it: 70 m wide at the front zone, 30 m at the knoll (Main3 2, item 10). A fire that starts by the road and runs west uphill on the east wind (the day-1 wind, Valley 5.3) narrows as it climbs and is held at the knoll. My pick. Other angle: lightning on the knoll running east; it fits the shape worse (it would be widest at the knoll). Grant.
2. L. Where it was held: the burn stops on a hard line at the knoll foot, not a fade. A cleared break: a strip of old cut stumps 4 to 6 m wide between the regrowth and the knoll's live firs, so the camp reads as the thing they saved. D: stumps saw-cut, greyed.
3. L. When: regrowth 4 to 6 m reads as about 10 to 15 years (unverified botany; Vesper checks it reads "years, not last summer"). Snags grey, not black. No ash on the ground by default (ForestPlan 6: GrassMud, no ash, day one).
4. L. The giants that lived through it: the Hollow Giant (202, 140) is a live giant on the burn's south edge with its base burned out by this fire. That is why it is hollow. Opening on the trail (E3).
5. L. The one that did not: the Gate Tree (290, 176), a dead giant cut to a 15 m stub as a hazard tree, the widest trunk in the burn. No other stub of its girth in view of it, so it stays the landmark. Trail within 2 m of its base.
6. D. Real burn traces, all old: fallen trunks that crossed the trail saw-cut, rounds rolled to the side (someone keeps this trail open); a hazard-tree sign at each burn trail mouth ("Burn area. Hazard trees. Stay on trail."); the trailhead board map at T and the burn-map board on Camp to J show the burn hatched, with a year. The year: Grant.

Foraging in the loop (DailyLoop 3, Valley 6.1)
7. L. What it is: Food for the day, near and uncertain, against the store, sure and far. Each dawn two of A, B and C bear, drawn at random. Draft: eaten at the patch, nothing carried (no stockpile, DECISIONS 2026-09-27). Day only; at night the patches do nothing.
8. L. Forage A, berries in the burn: low bushes (0.5 to 1.5 m) in a sunny opening on the trail edge, 29 m from Jg on Camp to Jg. Huckleberry is what grows back in an old burn (real). Bearing or bare reads from the trail at 10 m both ways, without stepping off: dark fruit, or stripped twigs. Pick from the trail edge.
9. L. A sits on the way to the store (Camp, A at 38 s, Jg, T, lot). That is its job: check A on the way out; if it bears, the store run can turn back at Jg. A bare A costs nothing extra if you were going to the lot anyway. Times: Camp to Jg 125 m, 50 s; Jg to T 105 m, 42 s. Sable confirms.
10. L. B and C are their own plants (Camp3Layout_Story 13, NorthLayout_Story 9), each told apart from A at a glance. Draft: B mushrooms on a rotting log in shade, C greens in the light gap at the fir wall foot. One event changes the fruit, so at least A must be berries; if B is not, that event runs at A only.
11. L. What goes wrong: the patch is bare (the other patch is a second try: A to B through camp about 136 m, 55 s); a patch picked over by something; a patch that bears wrong. And A sits on the two legs with the most events (item 13). No patch within 20 m of a search spot or an event site, so picking food is the only reason to stop there.
